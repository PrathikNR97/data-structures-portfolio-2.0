

* **1. The Question: Everyday Financial Challenges and Overspending**

* **Research Question:** To what extent does making more than three impulse purchases per week increase the average monthly overspending amount among U.S. adults aged 18 to 35?


* **Project Scope:** This project examines everyday financial challenges, focusing specifically on how frequent impulse purchases drive overspending and budgeting difficulties among average U.S. citizens.




* **2. Units and Variables**

* **Monthly Overspending Amount:** The difference between a person's planned monthly budget and actual spending, measured numerically in dollars (Calculated as: Overspending = Actual Monthly Spending - Planned Monthly Budget).


* **Frequency of Impulse Purchases:** A count variable measured as the number of unplanned purchases made per week, self-reported through survey questions such as: *"How many unplanned purchases did you make last week?"*

* **Age:** A numeric variable collected directly and filtered to include only respondents restricted to ages 18 to 35.


* **Income Level:** A categorical variable grouped into self-reported annual income brackets (e.g., <$30k, $30–60k, >$60k) used to help control for spending differences.




* **3. Data Collection and Sources**

* **Data Requirements:** Survey responses capturing weekly impulse purchases, monthly budget vs. actual spending data, and demographic information (age and income).


* **Potential Data Sources:**
* Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Expenditure Survey ([BLS CEX](https://www.bls.gov/cex/?utm_source=gemini))


* Kaggle datasets on personal finance and spending behavior


* UNC Charlotte library databases ([Library Databases](https://library.charlotte.edu/research-write/databases?utm_source=gemini))


* Self-created Qualtrics or Google Forms surveys distributed to students


* Pew Research datasets on consumer habits ([Pew Research](https://www.google.com/search?q=http://pewresearch.org/datasets/&utm_source=gemini))
